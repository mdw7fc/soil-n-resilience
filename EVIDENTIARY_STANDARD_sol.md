This standard was frozen before the code-wide parameter inventory and
parameter-extreme results were examined. It governs which results may remain
in the manuscript, Supplementary Information, response letter and
reproducibility summary.

## Scope: what counts as a live parameter

A live parameter is any primitive value that can change a model state,
calibration, scenario, forcing, weighting operation, reported statistic or
acceptance decision without changing program structure. The ledger must include:

1. global biophysical and crop parameters;
2. every regional biophysical and economic parameter;
3. monthly climate forcing, crop-calendar and calibration targets;
4. scenario magnitudes, durations, ceilings and recovery assumptions;
5. primitive price inputs and weighting quantities;
6. calibration bounds, solver tolerances and model run horizons;
7. uncertainty priors, random seeds, ensemble size and sensitivity ranges;
8. acceptance-test tolerances.

Pure plotting choices—colors, fonts, line widths, figure dimensions and label
positions—are not scientific parameters and are excluded. Their exclusion is
stated in the ledger metadata.

## Evidence classes

### Class A — central quantitative result

A result may be reported as a central quantitative finding only when:

- every primitive input is in the ledger with units, provenance, code location,
  calibration status and uncertainty treatment;
- all equations on its execution path pass dimensional checks;
- its no-shock baseline and relevant conservation/clearing identities pass;
- it is regenerated by deposited code from deposited inputs;
- repeated manuscript, SI, response-letter, table and figure values agree with
  the generator to the stated rounding precision;
- no unsupported or duplicate primitive independently specifies the same
  quantity.

### Class B — conditional scenario or sensitivity result

A result that satisfies reproducibility and dimensional requirements but
depends on an assumed, weakly supported or deliberately extreme parameter may
be reported only as a conditional scenario or sensitivity. The assumption must
be named adjacent to the result. It cannot be described as a forecast,
validation or empirically estimated central effect.

### Class C — contextual benchmark

A comparison with observed data may be called a benchmark when the observed
dataset is independent of the particular numerical output being compared and
the comparison metric is regenerated. It may be called a validation only when
the dataset was not used to calibrate any compared parameter, the observational
and modeled quantities are commensurate, and a prospective pass criterion was
specified. Comparisons that do not meet all three conditions remain benchmarks.

### Class D — exclude from the evidentiary chain

A numerical claim is excluded when it is non-regenerable, dimensionally
ambiguous, based on an unledgered primitive, dependent on duplicate conflicting
definitions, or presented at a stronger evidentiary level than its support.

## Prospective acceptance thresholds

- Numerical consistency: repeated percentages must agree with generated values
  within 0.05 percentage points; other quantities must agree within the final
  reported digit.
- Baseline invariance: maximum absolute yield-fraction drift under zero shock
  must be below 0.001 over 30 years for every region, both with behavioral
  channels disabled and under the full central S3 structure.
- Constrained clearing: maximum log-point market residual must be below 1e-10.
- Dimensional tests: every audited equation must reduce to its declared output
  units; all conversions must have one canonical factor.
- Numerical domain: every tested central, lower-bound and upper-bound run must
  be finite, with nonnegative fertilizer, yield, mineral N and SOM pools.
- Monotone focal mechanism: for the central model and declared parameter
  extremes, shock-induced yield loss at 50% regional-mean SOC must not be lower
  than at 150% SOC in any region.
- Robustness language: “robust” requires the claimed sign or ordering in every
  declared deterministic extreme and at least 95% of relevant Monte Carlo
  draws. Otherwise the result is described as parameter-sensitive.
- Cross-region financial ranking: requires comparable audited price primitives
  for all ranked regions. Four-region results may not be generalized to all
  eight.
- Validation language: no existing crop-response comparison is presumed to
  pass validation status. Each is evaluated under Class C after inventory.

## Decision record

Every retained numerical claim is assigned Class A, B or C in the final claim
register. Class D claims are listed with the reason for exclusion.
